## Written Resources

### Lighting, Mood & Composition in Environments (80.lv)
- **URL:** https://80.lv/articles/lighting-mood-composition-in-environments
- **Why:** Professional breakdown of how lighting and composition create mood
- **Key takeaways:**
  - Study films and classic paintings — many game principles come from there
  - Lighting serves both aesthetic and gameplay purposes
  - Composition guides the player's eye to what matters

### Exploring Lighting and Mood in Game Art (Ixie Gaming)
- **URL:** https://www.ixiegaming.com/blog/exploring-lighting-and-mood-in-game-art/
- **Why:** Covers how different lighting types create specific emotions
- **Key takeaways:**
  - **Ambient Light** — soft overall lighting, prevents total darkness
  - **Directional Light** — like sun/moon, creates long shadows
  - **Volumetric Lighting** — god rays, fog, atmospheric depth
  - Darkness feels mysterious/dangerous; ample light feels safe/welcoming

### Color Theory in Game Art (Pixune)
- **URL:** https://pixune.com/blog/color-theory-in-game-art-basics-and-complementary/
- **Why:** Understand how color choices create emotional response
- **Key takeaways:**
  - Warm colors = excitement, passion, urgency
  - Cool colors = serenity, mystery, fear
  - Work in grayscale first to nail values before adding color
  - Complementary colors (opposite on wheel) create high contrast

### The Level Design Book - Composition
- **URL:** https://book.leveldesignbook.com/process/blockout/massing/composition
- **Why:** Game-specific composition principles
- **Key takeaways:**
  - Readability is the most crucial aspect
  - Group shadow shapes into hierarchy (large to small)
  - If the audience doesn't understand it, the concept doesn't work

## Sketching Your Interpretation

After analysis, spend 10 minutes sketching ideas:

1. **Thumbnail compositions** (3-4 tiny sketches)
   - Same mood, different layout
   - What elements are essential vs. optional?

2. **Color palette extraction**
   - Pull 5-7 colors from reference
   - Consider: will you match or shift the palette?

3. **"What if" sketches**
   - What if it was YOUR world?
   - What elements from YOUR aesthetic could replace theirs?
   - Same mood, your voice

## Pro Tips

1. **Screenshot multiple states** — idle, action, transition
2. **Study at different sizes** — zoom out to see composition, zoom in for details
3. **Remove context** — flip the image, desaturate, blur — what still works?
4. **Compare to your own work** — what's the gap?
5. **Write, don't just look** — analysis sticks when you put it in words
